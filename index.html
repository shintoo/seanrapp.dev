Many years ago, I inadvertently saw what I thought was a shock video. In a factory, two workers dumped a box of live baby chicks into an industrial shredder. Though it was something one cannot un-see, I did my best to. I had no idea why anyone would do such a thing, beyond sickness and cruelty.

Turns out, they were doing their jobs. They worked at a farm that breeds hens for egg production - an industry that hatches 300 million hens each year in the US. Those baby chicks were males - they won't lay eggs, so they are waste.

On an egg farm, hens are killed after their egg production slows, around 1-2 years old. The farm then buys new hens to replace them from a hatchery. The hatchery, unfortunately, cannot choose what sex the baby chicks are born as. Half are male, half are female. The hatched eggs are transferred to a conveyor belt, where workers identify whether they are male or female. In some hatcheries, males are tossed to a separate conveyor belt, that leads to the shredder. In others, males are tossed into boxes, which will later be dumped into the shreddder.

Every single year in the US, over 300 million male baby chicks, alive, walking, fall in.

Around 7 billion chicks are killed in this way globally every year, to support egg production.

Animals are absolutely wonderful. We've learned a lot of really amazing things about them over the past several years. The world is just so beautiful.

  • Pigs were taught to operate a joystick to play a simple video game to win a treat, showing they have the capacity for understanding abstract cause-and-effect.
  • Honeybees succeeded in learning basic arithmetic, and symbolic representation of numbers.
  • Cows get happy when they learn and solve a puzzle.
  • A fish the size of your finger passes the mirror test, and recognizes themself in photographs
